The 15 interpersonal rules

Open any rule to read the reasoning behind it.

1Be responsible

Answer for your problems and don’t run from them. The more you face, the better a problem-solver you become.

2Choose your words carefully

Think before you speak. A house can be rebuilt, but a damaged relationship is harder to repair.

3Never be sarcastic

Humour turns into sarcasm when it crosses a line. Joke about situations, not about people.

4Practise humility

Education and experience without humility become ego. Soft-spoken people tend to be well regarded.

5Discuss but never argue

You can win an argument and still lose a friend, a job or respect.

6Stop playing the blame game

Your present is the result of your past, and your future is the result of your present.

7Never complain or criticise

Complaints produce excuses, not solutions. Be an example instead.

8Turn your promise into commitment

A promise is a feeling put into words. A commitment is carrying it out, legally and morally.

9Never gossip

Whoever speaks against someone in front of you will speak against you in front of others.

10Never bear grudges

Give it time, focus on your goal and let people go.

11Be grateful, never expect gratitude

Say thank you and sorry to the people close to you, and remember their favours.

12Be considerate

Give others the benefit of the doubt and assume a positive reason before you analyse their behaviour.

13Accept mistakes willingly and at once

Learning from a mistake is wise. Repeating it is the real failure.

14Think win-win

You’ll be happy and successful when you serve people and make sure others benefit too.

15Develop a sense of humour

Laughter is a free stress reliever. Keep yourself and the people around you happy.

Smart-12: professionalism

Professionalism is known as smartness in the international market. These twelve habits are the checklist.

Never avoid strangers

Be comfortable talking to anyone. You never know who you’ll need, or when.

Make a good first impression

Strangers form a view quickly. Keep the introduction brief and professional.

Never look down while speaking

Look at the person. Looking down signals low confidence.

Never slouch

Good posture looks confident and makes you appear taller.

Never frown

Smile, even in a bad mood. People like to spend time with lively people.

Be a charming conversationalist

Speak well rather than much, and talk about what the other person cares about.

Be a listener

People like to talk about themselves, and listening shows you what they plan next.

Stay in touch

Check in with acquaintances every couple of weeks and on special days.

Be a social star

Make time for gatherings and be there to welcome and see off friends.

Face your fears

Don’t advertise your weaknesses. Use logic and common sense, not emotion.

Be proactive

If something goes against your expectations, say so politely instead of blaming.

Be open-minded

Accept people as they are and respect their knowledge, not their caste, religion, gender or culture.

Kinesics: body language

Kinesics is the study of gesture, facial expression, posture and eye movement. Treat each cue as a hint and check it against context.

Handshake

Shows confidence and attitude.

Firm: confident. Weak: unsure. Palm down: controlling.

Arm crossing

Often defensiveness or discomfort.

Tight: disagreement. Loose: comfortable.

Eye contact

Interest, attention and confidence.

Direct: confident. Looking down: submissive.

Posture

Attitude and self-perception.

Upright: confident. Leaning in: interested.

Facial expression

The most expressive channel.

Raised brows: surprise. Lip biting: nerves.

Hand gestures

Emphasis, or nervousness.

Open palms: openness. Pointing: aggression.

Physiognomy

Physiognomy is the practice of judging character from the face and body. It sits in this guide as history and a talking point.

Read it as a prompt, not a verdict

Modern psychology doesn’t support judging personality from appearance. Use these ideas to become more observant, and never to screen or label people.
